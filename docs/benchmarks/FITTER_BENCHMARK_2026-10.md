# Fitter benchmark, October 2026

> **Note, 2026-10-28.** These numbers predate a fitter fix: size distributions are
> now integrated over mean ± 6σ (was sasmodels' default ±3σ, which cut the
> large-particle tail; see `src/analysis/model_fit.py`, `PD_NSIGMA`), and the
> benchmark's own curves are now generated over ± 6σ too. Re-run
> `python tools/fitter_benchmark.py` for current numbers; the effect is largest

Every fitting method in the Autonomous Analyser, scored on simulated SAXS curves
whose true structure is known. Produced by `tools/fitter_benchmark.py`
(logic: `src/fitting/benchmark.py`); plan: `docs/design/LLM_FITTER_PLAN.md`.

**Curves.** Generated by sasmodels from the five catalogue shapes in turn
(sphere, core-shell sphere, ellipsoid, cylinder, mass fractal), with random
realistic sizes, Schulz polydispersity 0.03 to 0.22, a flat background,
q from 0.04 to 0.12 up to 2.5 to 5 nm⁻¹, 180 to 320 points, and 0.8 to 6 %
Gaussian noise. "Practice" and "held-out" never share a curve; only the
held-out numbers judge a method.

**Scores.** Shape correct; size error on the equivalent radius the optimiser
receives (√(5/3)·Rg; the radius for a sphere; the primary radius for a mass
fractal); PDI error; shape accuracy among fits the method called confident
(≥ 0.5); seconds per curve on 4 cores. Sphere-only methods always answer
"sphere", so their shape accuracy is the fraction of spheres (20 %).

## What this shows

- **Catalogue + BIC** names the true structure in 40 of 40 held-out curves,
  with a median size error of 0.3 %. The two practice misses were a
  core-shell and a cylinder read as ellipsoids at high noise.
- The sphere fit and the ML regressor are right on spheres and report
  meaningless sizes for other shapes (a 50 to 3000 % error on rods and
  aggregates), which is what reaches the optimiser today when the product is
  not spherical.
- Catalogue + BIC costs about 8 to 9 s per curve against 0.2 s.

Statistics alone fall to 55 % of shapes and 52 % of sizes within 10 %.
Subtraction errors and missing q-ranges are handled well; interactions,
aggregation and mixtures are not, and real spheres are often over-read as a
more flexible core-shell or fractal model. This is the set the LLM-assisted
fitter must improve on:

```
python tools/fitter_benchmark.py --n 40 --split hard_heldout --methods catalogue_bic llm_assisted
```

(needs the AI login; about one AI call per curve, the knowledge bundle cached).

## Limits of this benchmark

The clean sets have one shape per curve and perfect subtraction, where
statistics alone already find the shape. The hard sets add the common
real-data problems but are still simulated: no instrument smearing, no
detector artefacts, and shapes only from the catalogue. Real beamtime curves
with expert verdicts (the `ai_knowledge/cases/` library) are the final test.

## Reproduce

```
python tools/fitter_benchmark.py --n 40 --split heldout
python tools/fitter_benchmark.py --n 40 --split practice
```

Results go to `logs/fitter_benchmark/` (JSONL per case and method, a Markdown
report, and a scores JSON); a stopped run resumes where it left off.
